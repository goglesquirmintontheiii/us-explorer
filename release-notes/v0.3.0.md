Phase 3: roads, bridges and roadside objects.

**New**

- Every road, street, path and railway from OpenStreetMap, built in 3D around you as you go (about 2 km out on High; less on Low and Medium so laptops keep up). Map data is cached on disk in a compact form, so places you've been load instantly
- Roads sit properly on the ground: the terrain is levelled under every road with cut and fill, blending back to the natural slope beside it, and roads re-settle as more detailed terrain arrives
- Junctions are one merged surface with rounded curb corners, with no overlapping or flickering roads
- Lane markings from the map's lane data: double yellow and dashed centre lines, lane lines, edge lines, turn arrows, continental crosswalks and stop lines
- Sidewalks and curbs on city streets, following the corners
- Bridges with decks, parapets and piers, kept clear of the roads and railways they cross; approaches climb and dip at a highway grade. Freeway interchanges stack ramps at their real levels, and you can walk on every deck
- Tunnels with tiled, lit walls and portal headwalls. The hillside opens at the portals, and you can walk through
- Street name signs at intersections with the real names; Interstate, US and state route shields with real numbers; green exit signs at freeway exits; speed limit signs
- Traffic signals on mast arms that cycle through their phases, pedestrian signals (walk, flashing hand), stop and yield signs. They come from the map, or are placed the way they usually are where the map doesn't say
- Streetlights that come on at dusk and light the street, guardrails along bridge approaches and embankments, cones and barrels in work zones, licence-plate camera posts where mapped, utility poles, fire hydrants, mailboxes, bus stops with shelters and benches, benches and bollards
- Road wear: potholes and patches, more on rough and minor rural roads (potholes will fill with water when rain arrives)
- Surfaces from the map: asphalt, concrete, pavers, gravel, dirt and boardwalks; railways with ballast, sleepers and rails
- You bump into poles, posts and hydrants instead of walking through them
- Settings → Roads turns all of this off if you just want the land

**From the v0.1.0 checklist notes**

- Shadows: the sun casts real shadows from mountains, cliffs, bridges, signs, poles and you. The distance and quality follow the graphics preset, and Settings → Shadows turns them off
- The sea looks like water now: moving waves that catch the sun and reflect the sky, deeper water darker and bluer, whitecaps and foam along the shore
- Faster loading: detailed lidar elevation comes in one request per area instead of up to 21, and imagery downloads at the same time as elevation instead of after it, so new places sharpen up much sooner
- The F3 overlay shows your hardware: CPU and cores, RAM, GPU and graphics API, the game's CPU and memory use, and live download and disk speeds
- Offline downloads now include lidar elevation and road data, so a downloaded area works fully without a connection

**Known limits**

- Buildings come in Phase 4. Until then city blocks are open ground between the streets
- Roads load from public OpenStreetMap servers. When they're busy a tile can take a while, and it retries by itself
- Some freeway signs and inferred stop signs won't match reality everywhere; mapped signs always win
